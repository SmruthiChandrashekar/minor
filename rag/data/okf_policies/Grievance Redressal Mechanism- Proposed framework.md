1. Background & Intent
As Puravankara continues to strengthen its governance, people practices, and stakeholder engagement, there is a clear opportunity to institutionalise a unified Grievance Redressal Mechanism (GRM) across the organisation.
The intent is to:
Move towards policy automation and digital governance
Establish a single source of truth for all formal company policies
Ensure consistent, transparent, and timely grievance handling across all stakeholder groups
Align with evolving expectations under ESG, BRSR, labour laws, and best-in-class corporate governance practices
This note outlines a proposed framework for how a grievance redressal mechanism for Puravankara could be designed and operationalised.

2. Policy Automation & Single Policy Repository
Puravankara is in the process of creating an automated policy framework, which will serve as:
The repository for all approved company policies
A structured platform for policy access, acknowledgement, and compliance tracking
A common interface for policy-linked workflows, including grievance reporting
Policies already disclosed on the public domain (as available on the Investors page:https://www.puravankara.com/investors) can be:
Listed and referenced within this system
Clearly demarcated as public disclosures versus internal governance policies
Linked to relevant grievance categories where applicable (e.g., whistleblower, code of conduct, HR policies)
The grievance redressal mechanism is proposed to be a core, cross-cutting policy within this automated framework.

3. Scope of the Grievance Redressal Mechanism
The grievance redressal policy is proposed to be inclusive and stakeholder-centric, extending beyond employees alone.

Stakeholder Coverage
External Stakeholders
Anyone working in the geography of the project site and in general if they have been aggrieved by the actions of the company
Internal Stakeholders
Employees (permanent, probationary, trainees)
Senior management and leadership
Workforce Engaged Through Contractors
Contract labour at project sites
Facility management staff
Security, housekeeping, and other outsourced personnel

4. What Qualifies as a Grievance
A grievance may be defined as:
“Any formal complaint, concern, or dissatisfaction raised by a stakeholder, arising out of Puravankara’s business operations, employment practices, contractual arrangements, or service delivery, where the stakeholder believes they have been unfairly treated or adversely impacted.”
Illustrative Categories of Grievances
For External Stakeholders
Ethical concerns or misconduct by company representatives
For Employees
Workplace conduct and behavioural issues
Harassment, discrimination, or unfair treatment
Health, safety, and working conditions
Policy violations or ethical concerns
For Contract Workers
Wage payments or statutory benefits
Unsafe working conditions
Excessive working hours
Harassment or mistreatment at site
Non-compliance with labour standards by contractors
Matters already under active litigation or statutory investigation may be excluded or handled through defined legal channels, while still allowing reporting for record and monitoring purposes.

5. Grievance Reporting Channels
The mechanism should enable safe, accessible, and confidential reporting, with multiple entry points depending on the stakeholder group.
Existing Reporting Channels
Digital Platform (Primary Channel)Integrated through the policy automation framework and, for employees, through the GreytHR HRMS
Email-based ReportingDedicated grievance email IDs mapped to categories
Physical / On-site Reporting (for contract workers)Site-level grievance registers or facilitation through site HR / safety officers
Escalation / Whistleblower ChannelFor serious ethical, legal, or governance-related grievances
Proposed Reporting Channels:
A hyperlink or QR code will activate a chatbot-based grievance response system. Based on responses to a set of guided questions, the system will process the information and facilitate filing of the complaint, either manually or through an automated workflow. Each complaint will be mapped to the relevant business process and routed to the appropriate responsible authority.
For instance, in the case of a delayed payment, the complainant can submit the required details through the chatbot, and the matter will be redirected to the concerned business head for resolution.
Complaints that fall outside established business processes will be escalated to a decision-making engine built on predefined policies and governance frameworks. For example, issues such as unfair trade practices in procurement, wage denial at a site, or harassment concerns will trigger a formal grievance registration, followed by a structured response and redressal mechanism.
Advanced feature: The system can also enable grievance resolution through the Microsoft Teams platform (or an equivalent interface), driven by the integrated decision engine.
